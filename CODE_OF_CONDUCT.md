# CODE OF CONDUCT

> `// SDT NETWORK INTERACTION PROTOCOL`
>
> The network is open.
> Act like someone worth having on it.

---

## `01 // OUR STANDARD`

Super Duper Techno is built around creativity, experimentation,
technology, games, and original ideas.

We want people with different skills, backgrounds, opinions,
and approaches to be able to work together.

Participation in the SDT community means treating other people
with respect.

---

## `02 // EXPECTED BEHAVIOR`

Community members are expected to:

- Be respectful and constructive.
- Assume good intentions where reasonable.
- Give useful criticism.
- Accept criticism without personal attacks.
- Respect different skill levels and experiences.
- Keep technical disagreements focused on the problem.
- Credit other people's work.
- Respect project licenses and intellectual property.
- Help maintain a welcoming environment.
- Take responsibility for mistakes and correct them when possible.

You don't have to agree with everyone.

You do have to treat them like a person.

---

## `03 // UNACCEPTABLE BEHAVIOR`

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment.
- Threats or intimidation.
- Personal attacks.
- Hate speech or discriminatory behavior.
- Sexual harassment.
- Deliberate humiliation or targeting of individuals.
- Doxxing or exposing private information.
- Publishing someone's private communications without permission.
- Malicious impersonation.
- Trolling intended to disrupt the community.
- Repeatedly derailing technical discussions.
- Deliberate attempts to damage projects or community infrastructure.
- Unauthorized access to accounts, systems, or data.

---

## `04 // DISAGREEMENTS`

Technical disagreement is normal.

Creative disagreement is normal.

Arguments about implementation are normal.

What's important is **how the disagreement happens**.

Good:

> "I don't think this approach will scale because..."

Better:

> "I tested another approach and it reduced the problem by..."

Not acceptable:

> "You're an idiot."

Challenge ideas.

Don't attack people.

---

## `05 // PROJECT-SPECIFIC RULES`

Individual SDT repositories may have additional rules.

Always follow the rules defined by the project you're participating in.

If a repository has its own:

- README
- CONTRIBUTING.md
- SECURITY.md
- Development guidelines
- Community rules

those documents should be reviewed before contributing.

---

## `06 // REPORTING PROBLEMS`

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it
to the project maintainers.

For security vulnerabilities, **do not use a public issue**.

Follow the instructions in `SECURITY.md` instead.

For general community issues, use the appropriate communication
channel associated with the project.

---

## `07 // ENFORCEMENT`

Project maintainers may take appropriate action when this Code
of Conduct is violated.

Depending on the situation, actions may include:

- A warning.
- Removal of inappropriate content.
- Temporary restriction of participation.
- Removal from a project.
- Permanent removal from the community.
- Reporting serious incidents to the appropriate authorities
  when necessary.

The response will depend on the severity and circumstances
of the incident.

---

## `08 // GOOD-FAITH PARTICIPATION`

Mistakes happen.

People misunderstand things.

People occasionally say something poorly.

Not every disagreement is misconduct.

Moderation should be reasonable and focused on maintaining
a productive community.

If someone makes a genuine mistake, correction and learning
should be preferred where appropriate.

---

## `09 // THE SDT RULE`

There is one principle behind all of this:

> **Creativity and fun over standard edition.**

That means:

Build weird things.

Ask questions.

Experiment.

Disagree.

Learn.

Break things responsibly.

Fix them.

And let other people do the same.
